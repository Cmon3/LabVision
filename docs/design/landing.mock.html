Counts decide whether a batch is released.

In pharma quality control, food and water labs, trained people still count colonies on every plate by eye. Inspectors have repeatedly cited plate-reading errors, and small colonies are easy to miss. Large labs buy closed automated stations; most labs do not have one.

How it works

01 · Ask

Photo and a question

Upload, use a fixed camera, or drop photos in an inbox. Type or say “count colonies larger than 2 mm”.

02 · Agent works

Plan, find, measure, verify

Muse Spark Open plans. SAM 3.1 finds the colonies. Python measures. The report is refused if any number disagrees.

03 · You decide

Review by exception

Only flagged plates need you. Draw a box to fix a missed colony, approve, export to CSV for your LIMS.

What it won’t do

  • Name organisms. The model declined for safety reasons, and we kept that line.
  • Flag mixed cultures. It did not beat a simple baseline, so a rule set in advance removed it.
  • Count plates above 300 colonies. Those are reported as too numerous to count: dilute and re-plate.
